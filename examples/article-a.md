# From a demonstration to a checkable claim

A model demonstration can look convincing while leaving the action-to-outcome contract unclear. This authored article explains a small evaluation example. The media below are generated test patterns, not evidence of an agent failure or a repaired system.

Imagine a visual agent that should wait for an eligible turn before responding. A selected clip may illustrate movement, but a caption cannot establish why it happened. We need to separate a visible label from independently checked behavior.

First record the trigger and expected response. Then reproduce the same initial state, capture the relevant interval, and inspect every source frame needed for the claim. A sampled still can support a local observation; it cannot establish that an event never occurred between samples.

These moving test patterns make playback and clock accounting inspectable. They do not show an actual game, a model acting in an environment, or measured reader attention. Muted video and sound-enabled observation are separate conditions.

When reading and playback overlap, elapsed active time is the union of those intervals. Seeking advances the media position but does not mean the skipped footage was watched. Time spent calling a model belongs to the system clock rather than the simulated reader clock.

A useful editorial choice would bring the concrete check earlier and remove repetition. A model can propose that change, but only a controlled real-reader study can show whether comprehension or completion improves.

The next step is to test the proposed check and measure real readers under declared conditions. Keep failure records, unvisited sections, censoring and missing feedback visible. Finishing a post does not establish comprehension.
